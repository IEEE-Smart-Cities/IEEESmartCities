Call for Speakers

Share your work at IEEE Smart Cities

We invite industry professionals, researchers, public-sector leaders, educators, entrepreneurs, students, Women in Engineering (WIE), Young Professionals (YP), and community stakeholders to submit session proposals for IEEE Smart Cities. This is an in-person regional event designed to share practical insights, case studies, emerging research, tools, and lessons learned across the smart-city ecosystem — from semiconductors and AI infrastructure to energy, mobility, governance, and community impact.

Section 2

Who should submit

You do not need to be an IEEE member, an academic, or a previous conference speaker to propose a session. We are looking for people doing the work.

We welcome proposals from people working in industry, startups, research, education, government, nonprofit organizations, and community programs. Students, first-time speakers, Women in Engineering members, and Young Professionals are encouraged to submit when they have relevant technical work or an applied project to share.

Section 5

What to prepare before you submit

Have these ready before you open the submission portal. Most applicants need 20–30 minutes to complete a proposal.

  • Session title — short and public-facing.
  • Abstract — 150–300 words.
  • Speaker bio — 100–150 words.
  • Topic track — one of the eight tracks above.
  • Presentation approach — technical presentation or applied case study.
  • In-person availability — confirmation that you can present in Plano.
  • Audience takeaways — 3–5 bullet points.
  • Target audience and technical level.
  • Primary UN SDG plus a 75–150 word explanation of the connection.
  • LinkedIn profile or website — recommended.
  • Headshot — optional now, required if accepted.
  • Prior speaking link or short video pitch — optional.

Every proposal should identify at least one UN Sustainable Development Goal and briefly explain the connection. A short, specific explanation is more useful than a general one.

Section 6

Submission requirements

Each proposal should include a presentation title, 150–300 word abstract, speaker bio, topic track, presentation approach, audience level, 3–5 attendee takeaways, and a brief explanation of how the session connects to at least one UN Sustainable Development Goal. LinkedIn profiles, prior speaking links, headshots, and short video pitches are optional but welcome. Commercial sales pitches will not be accepted.

Section 7

How proposals are reviewed

Each proposal is assigned to an independent reviewer who applies the rubric below. The review considers relevance to the event theme, practical value to attendees, speaker experience, originality of the topic, and clarity of the abstract and learning outcomes. Organizers use the completed reviews to make final program decisions.

Criterion Weight What reviewers look for
Relevance to smart cities and the event theme 25% Fits one or more event tracks and clearly connects to smart cities, infrastructure, technology, sustainability, community impact, or “chips to cities.”
Practical value to attendees 25% Offers lessons learned, frameworks, case studies, tools, or actionable insight rather than generic statements.
Speaker credibility and experience 20% Relevant professional, academic, public-sector, student, startup, or community experience appropriate to the session.
Originality and freshness 15% A timely, distinctive, cross-disciplinary, or emerging perspective; avoids generic or recycled content.
Clarity of abstract and takeaways 15% Title, abstract, and learning outcomes are understandable, structured, and appropriate for the stated audience.

Each criterion is scored 1–5, where 5 is a strong accept and 1 is not appropriate for this program. Depending on program fit and available space, organizers may accept, waitlist, request a revision to, or decline a proposal. A strong topic from a first-time speaker is welcome — a clear abstract with specific takeaways matters more than a long speaking history.

No sales pitches: sessions must be educational. A proposal centered on a product or service may be declined or returned for revision as an educational case study.

Section 8

What happens after acceptance

Accepted speakers confirm participation by October 16, 2026, provide final speaker details by October 30, 2026, and submit final presentation slides by November 6, 2026. Final slide revisions are due no later than November 18, 2026.

  1. Confirm your participation by Friday, October 16, 2026, 5:00 PM CT — including your final session details and in-person availability.
  2. Send final speaker material by Friday, October 30, 2026 — website-ready bio, headshot, and final session title and description.
  3. Submit final slides by Friday, November 6, 2026, 5:00 PM CT. The committee retains a backup copy to protect event-day continuity.
  4. Confirm recording permission — optional. Sessions are only recorded or shared with your written consent.
  5. Keep the session educational — no commercial sales pitches, as agreed at submission.
  6. Send minor slide corrections by Wednesday, November 18, 2026, 5:00 PM CT. No major content changes after this point.

Accepted speakers will receive check-in, room, and audiovisual instructions before the in-person event at Collin College – Plano Campus Conference Center.
