Call for Speakers

Share your work at IEEE Smart Cities Dallas

We invite industry professionals, researchers, public-sector leaders, educators, entrepreneurs, students, Women in Engineering (WIE), Young Professionals (YP), and community stakeholders to submit speaking proposals for IEEE Smart Cities Dallas. Who should submit

You do not need to be an IEEE member, an academic, or a previous conference speaker to propose a session. We are looking for people doing the work.

🏢

Industry & startups

Engineers, architects, product and program leaders, and founders building smart-city technology, infrastructure, or services.

🏫

Research & education

Researchers, faculty, educators, and graduate students working on any layer of the smart-city stack.

🏛

Public sector

City, county, regional, and agency leaders responsible for planning, operations, utilities, transportation, or digital services.

🎓

Students

Undergraduate and graduate students — the student lightning talk format is built for first-time speakers.

👥

WIE & YP

Women in Engineering (WIE) and Young Professionals (YP) members, with dedicated sessions in the program.

🤝

Community stakeholders

Nonprofit, civic, and community organizations working on equity, access, workforce, and local impact.

Section 3

Proposal types and session lengths

Choose the format that fits your material. The committee may suggest a different format — for example, converting a talk into a panel contribution — to balance the program.

Format Length Best for
Technical talk 20–25 min Methods, architectures, results, and engineering detail.
Industry case study 20–25 min A real deployment: what you built, what it cost, what went wrong, what you learned.
Panel discussion 40–45 min A cross-disciplinary question that benefits from several perspectives.
Workshop or tutorial 45–60 min Hands-on skills, tooling walkthroughs, and frameworks attendees can apply.
Demonstration or showcase 10–15 min Showing a working system, prototype, or dataset live.
Student lightning talk 5–10 min Student projects, thesis work, and competition results. Ideal for a first talk.
Young Professional or WIE session 20–45 min Career, leadership, mentorship, and community-building sessions.
Virtual talk 20–25 min Any of the above delivered online — see the hybrid requirements below.
Speaker-only cycle. We are not running a paper submission or peer-review process this cycle. There is no manuscript, no proceedings, and no publication requirement — you are proposing a session, not a paper.

Section 5

What to prepare before you submit

Have these ready before you open the submission portal. Most applicants need 20–30 minutes to complete a proposal.

  • Session title — short and public-facing.
  • Abstract — 150–300 words.
  • Speaker bio — 100–150 words.
  • Topic track — one of the eight tracks above.
  • Proposal type and duration — see the format table.
  • Delivery mode — in person, virtual, or either.
  • Audience takeaways — 3–5 bullet points.
  • Target audience and technical level.
  • Primary UN SDG plus a 75–150 word explanation of the connection.
  • LinkedIn profile or website — recommended.
  • Headshot — optional now, required if accepted.
  • Prior speaking link or short video pitch — optional.
UN SDG alignment. Every proposal identifies at least one UN Sustainable Development Goal and briefly explains the connection between that goal and the proposed session. This is how the program connects technical work to community and global impact — a short, specific explanation is worth more than a long, general one.
Section 6

Submission requirements

Each proposal should include a presentation title, 150–300 word abstract, speaker bio, topic track, session format, audience level, 3–5 attendee takeaways, and a brief explanation of how the session connects to at least one UN Sustainable Development Goal. LinkedIn profiles, prior speaking links, headshots, and short video pitches are optional but welcome. Commercial sales pitches will not be accepted.

Section 7

How proposals are reviewed

Proposals are reviewed by the Technical Program Committee using the rubric below, based on relevance to the event theme, practical value to attendees, speaker experience, originality of the topic, and clarity of the abstract and learning outcomes. Each proposal receives at least two independent reviews before the committee decision.

Criterion Weight What reviewers look for
Relevance to smart cities and the event theme 25% Fits one or more event tracks and clearly connects to smart cities, infrastructure, technology, sustainability, community impact, or “chips to cities.”
Practical value to attendees 25% Offers lessons learned, frameworks, case studies, tools, demonstrations, or actionable insight rather than generic statements.
Speaker credibility and experience 20% Relevant professional, academic, public-sector, student, startup, or community experience appropriate to the session.
Originality and freshness 15% A timely, distinctive, cross-disciplinary, or emerging perspective; avoids generic or recycled content.
Clarity of abstract and takeaways 15% Title, abstract, and learning outcomes are understandable, structured, and appropriate for the stated audience.

Each criterion is scored 1–5, where 5 is a strong accept and 1 is not appropriate for this program. Depending on program fit, the committee may accept a proposal as an individual talk, a panel contribution, a workshop, a demo, or a lightning talk, or place it on the waitlist. A strong topic from a first-time speaker is welcome — a clear abstract with specific takeaways matters more than a long speaking history.

No sales pitches. Sessions must be educational. Proposals that are primarily a product or service pitch may be declined regardless of score, or returned with a request to reframe the material as an educational case study.
Section 8

What happens after acceptance

Accepted speakers confirm participation by October 16, 2026, provide final speaker details by October 30, 2026, and submit final presentation slides by November 6, 2026. Virtual speakers complete a technology check between November 9 and November 12, 2026. Final slide revisions are due no later than November 18, 2026.

  1. Confirm your participation by Friday, October 16, 2026, 5:00 PM CT — including your session format and whether you will present in person or online.
  2. Send final speaker material by Friday, October 30, 2026 — website-ready bio, headshot, and final session title and description.
  3. Submit final slides by Friday, November 6, 2026, 5:00 PM CT. The committee retains a backup copy to protect event-day continuity.
  4. Complete a technology check between November 9 and November 12, 2026 if you are presenting online or joining a hybrid panel.
  5. Confirm recording permission — optional. Sessions are only recorded or shared with your written consent.
  6. Keep the session educational — no commercial sales pitches, as agreed at submission.
  7. Send minor slide corrections by Wednesday, November 18, 2026, 5:00 PM CT. No major content changes after this point.

Presenting online
