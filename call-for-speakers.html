Call for Speakers

Share your work at IEEE Smart Cities

We invite industry professionals, researchers, public-sector leaders, educators, entrepreneurs, students, Women in Engineering (WIE), Young Professionals (YP), and community stakeholders to submit session proposals for IEEE Smart Cities. This is an hybrid regional conference designed to share practical insights, case studies, emerging research, tools, and lessons learned across the smart-city ecosystem — from semiconductors and AI infrastructure to energy, mobility, governance, and community impact.

Who should submit

We welcome practitioners from industry, startups, research, education, government, nonprofits, and community programs. IEEE membership or prior speaking experience is not required; students, first-time speakers, Women in Engineering members, and Young Professionals are encouraged to submit relevant technical work or applied projects.

Session proposals

Submit a hybrid technical presentation or case study. Final session lengths and the detailed program structure will be confirmed after proposals are selected.

Proposal Focus
Technical presentation Methods, architectures, results, engineering detail, or emerging research.
Applied case study A real deployment: what you built, what it cost, what went wrong, what you learned.

What to prepare before you submit

Have these ready before you open the submission portal. Most applicants need 20–30 minutes to complete a proposal.

  • Session title — short and public-facing.
  • Abstract — 150–300 words.
  • Speaker bio — 100–150 words.
  • Topic track — the closest match for your proposal.
  • Presentation approach — technical presentation or applied case study.
  • Participation format — in-person, virtual, or recorded.
  • Audience takeaways — 3–5 bullet points.
  • Target audience and technical level.
  • Primary UN SDG plus a 75–150 word explanation of the connection.
  • LinkedIn profile or website — recommended.
  • Headshot — optional now, required if accepted.
  • Prior speaking link or short video pitch — optional.

Every proposal should identify at least one UN Sustainable Development Goal and briefly explain the connection. A short, specific explanation is more useful than a general one.

How proposals are reviewed

Each proposal is assigned to an independent reviewer who applies the rubric below. The review considers relevance to the conference theme, practical value to attendees, speaker experience, originality of the topic, and clarity of the abstract and learning outcomes. Organizers use the completed reviews to make final program decisions.

Criterion Weight What reviewers look for
Relevance to smart cities and the conference theme 25% Fits one or more conference tracks and clearly connects to smart cities, infrastructure, technology, sustainability, community impact, or “chips to cities.”
Practical value to attendees 25% Offers lessons learned, frameworks, case studies, tools, or actionable insight rather than generic statements.
Speaker credibility and experience 20% Relevant professional, academic, public-sector, student, startup, or community experience appropriate to the session.
Originality and freshness 15% A timely, distinctive, cross-disciplinary, or emerging perspective; avoids generic or recycled content.
Clarity of abstract and takeaways 15% Title, abstract, and learning outcomes are understandable, structured, and appropriate for the stated audience.

Each criterion is scored 1–5, where 5 is a strong accept and 1 is not appropriate for this program. Depending on program fit and available space, organizers may accept, waitlist, request a revision to, or decline a proposal. A strong topic from a first-time speaker is welcome — a clear abstract with specific takeaways matters more than a long speaking history.

No sales pitches: sessions must be educational. A proposal centered on a product or service may be declined or returned for revision as an educational case study.

What happens after acceptance

Accepted speakers confirm participation by October 16, 2026 and submit final presentation materials by November 6, 2026. Virtual or recorded presentations are due by November 10, 2026.

  1. Confirm your participation by October 16, 2026, including your final session details and participation format.
  2. Submit final presentation materials by November 6, 2026, including final slides and website-ready speaker information.
  3. Submit virtual or recorded presentations by November 10, 2026, if applicable.
  4. Confirm recording permission — optional. Sessions are only recorded or shared with your written consent.
  5. Keep the session educational — no commercial sales pitches, as agreed at submission.

Accepted speakers will receive check-in, room, and audiovisual instructions before the conference. Venue and access details will be shared with confirmed speakers.
