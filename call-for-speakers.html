Call for Speaker Proposals

The conference invites industry professionals, researchers, public-sector leaders, educators, entrepreneurs, students, Women in Engineering (WIE), Young Professionals (YP), and community stakeholders to submit session proposals. This hybrid regional conference presents practical insights, case studies, emerging research, tools, and documented outcomes across the smart-city ecosystem — from semiconductors and AI infrastructure to energy, mobility, governance, and community impact.

Eligibility

The conference welcomes practitioners from industry, startups, research, education, government, nonprofits, and community programs. IEEE membership or prior speaking experience is not required; students, first-time speakers, Women in Engineering members, and Young Professionals are encouraged to submit relevant technical contributions or applied projects.

Presentation Formats

Review Process and Evaluation Criteria

Each proposal is assigned to an independent reviewer who applies the rubric below. The review considers relevance to the conference theme, practical value to attendees, speaker experience, originality of the topic, and clarity of the abstract and learning outcomes. Organizers use the completed reviews to make final program decisions.

Criterion Weight Evaluation considerations
Relevance to smart cities and the conference theme 25% Fits one or more conference tracks and clearly connects to smart cities, infrastructure, technology, sustainability, community impact, or “chips to cities.”
Practical value to attendees 25% Offers lessons learned, frameworks, case studies, tools, or actionable insight rather than generic statements.
Speaker credibility and experience 20% Relevant professional, academic, public-sector, student, startup, or community experience appropriate to the session.
Originality and freshness 15% A timely, distinctive, cross-disciplinary, or emerging perspective; avoids generic or recycled content.
Clarity of abstract and takeaways 15% Title, abstract, and learning outcomes are understandable, structured, and appropriate for the stated audience.

Each criterion is scored 1–5, where 5 is a strong accept and 1 is not appropriate for this program. Depending on program fit and available space, organizers may accept, waitlist, request a revision to, or decline a proposal. Proposals from first-time speakers receive full consideration; a clear abstract with specific takeaways matters more than a long speaking history.

Non-commercial content requirement: Presentations must be educational. Proposals centered on a product or service may be declined or returned for revision as an educational case study.

Requirements for Accepted Speakers

Accepted speakers confirm participation by October 16, 2026 and submit final presentation materials by November 6, 2026. Virtual or recorded presentations are due by November 10, 2026.

  1. Confirm participation by October 16, 2026, including final session details and participation format.
  2. Submit final presentation materials by November 6, 2026, including final slides and website-ready speaker information.
  3. Submit virtual or recorded presentations by November 10, 2026, if applicable.
  4. Confirm recording permission — optional. Sessions are recorded or distributed only with written consent.
  5. Keep the session educational — no commercial sales pitches, as agreed at submission.

Accepted speakers will receive check-in, room, and audiovisual instructions before the conference. Venue and access details will be shared with confirmed speakers.
