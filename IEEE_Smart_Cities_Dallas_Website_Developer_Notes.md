## 1. Overall Direction

- Event name: **IEEE Smart Cities Dallas**
- Proposed theme: *From Chips to Cities: Building Smart, Resilient, and Connected Communities*
- Proposed tagline: Connecting semiconductors, AI, data centers, infrastructure, energy, mobility, and public services for the future of smart cities.
- The website should look professional, simple, and suitable for an IEEE technical event.
- The event is speaker-only for this cycle. We are not planning a paper submission process this year.

- Invite industry professionals, researchers, public-sector leaders, students, WIE, YP, and community stakeholders.

## 7. Suggested Speaker Proposal Form Fields

- Full name, email, phone number
- Organization and job title
- Speaker category: industry, academic, government/public agency, student, YP, WIE, startup, community organization, other
- Proposed session title
- Preferred topic track
- Proposal type
- Abstract or summary: 150-300 words
- 3-5 audience takeaways
- Technical level: introductory, intermediate, advanced
- Preferred format: in-person, virtual, hybrid, flexible
- Primary UN SDG
- Additional UN SDGs, if applicable
- Short explanation of SDG alignment: 75-150 words
- Short speaker bio: 100-150 words
- Headshot or LinkedIn link, optional
- Open to joining a panel, optional
- Willing to mentor students/YP, optional

## 8. Proposal Form and Tracking Connection

- Recommended for this year: Microsoft Form or Google Form linked from the website.
- Reason: this is a speaker-only event and we need a simple, fast workflow.
- Website button: **Submit Speaker Proposal**
- Button opens the form.
- Form responses go to Excel or Google Sheets.
- The spreadsheet becomes the proposal tracker and review matrix.
- Confirmed speakers can later be added manually to the website Speakers and Agenda pages.

## 9. Simple Workflow

1. Speaker visits website.
2. Speaker clicks Submit Speaker Proposal.
3. Speaker completes form.
4. Response goes into Excel/Google Sheet.
5. Technical Program team assigns reviewer or committee owner.
6. Reviewer scores proposal using agreed rubric.
7. Committee marks decision: accept, panel, workshop, revise, waitlist, or decline.
8. Accepted speaker receives confirmation email.
9. Website is updated with speaker name, topic, and agenda placement.

## 10. Tool Options to Discuss

### Microsoft Forms + Excel

- Good option if the chapter/committee uses Microsoft 365.
- Simple and professional.
- Works well with Excel tracking.
- Access permissions should be checked for external reviewers.

### Google Forms + Google Sheets

- Fastest and easiest to launch.
- Good for quick collaboration and filtering.
- Less formal, but sufficient for speaker-only event.

### Website Form

- Looks more integrated and professional.
- Developer must confirm where responses are stored and how they are exported.
- Needs spam protection and reliable export to Excel/CSV.

### EasyChair or CMT

- Useful for formal academic submissions and paper review.
- Probably heavier than needed for this speaker-only event.
- May create extra steps for industry speakers because they may need accounts.
- Can be considered later if the event becomes a paper conference.

## 11. Review Criteria

Use 1-5 scoring for each criterion. Suggested criteria:

- Relevance to IEEE Smart Cities
- Alignment with UN SDGs
- Technical or professional value
- Practical impact and real-world applicability
- Speaker credibility
- Clarity and preparedness
- Inclusion, local relevance, and community value
